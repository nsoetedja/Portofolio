Projects

Cameco Corporation (CCJ) — DCF Equity Research
A two-scenario DCF valuation of the world’s largest uranium producer, built on a full 3-statement model with live sensitivity analysis.
DCF UFCF WACC 3-Statement Model Scenario Analysis Sensitivity Analysis Equity Research Excel IFRS Commodities

Cameco Corporation is the world’s largest publicly traded uranium producer, operating the highest-grade uranium mine on earth — McArthur River — and holding a 49% stake in Westinghouse Electric, the dominant Western nuclear services company. With nuclear energy re-emerging as the only zero-carbon baseload power source capable of meeting AI data center demand and net-zero targets, Cameco sits at the intersection of a supply-constrained commodity and a structural policy tailwind.

The model covers five years of historical actuals (2021A–2025A) and nine years of projections (2027E–2035E) across a full Income Statement, Balance Sheet, and Cash Flow statement. Valuation uses Unlevered Free Cash Flow (UFCF) with mid-year discounting and a WACC built from CAPM. Two scenarios — Base and Upside — toggle uranium market share, Westinghouse dividend growth, terminal growth rate, and beta from a single switch on the Cover sheet.

The Base Case yields ~$50 CDN/share; the Upside Case yields ~$94 CDN, against a current market price of ~$139 CDN. Rather than forcing the model to match the market, the analysis treats the gap as a strategic scarcity premium — compensation for Cameco’s irreplaceable asset base, long-term contracting optionality, and nuclear sentiment that a 9-year DCF cannot fully capture.

Key Modeling Decisions

  • Westinghouse modeled as IFRS equity-accounted investment — only actual cash dividends enter UFCF, not non-cash equity earnings
  • Beta toggled by scenario: 1.15 (base) / 1.00 (upside), anchored to Cameco’s observed market beta of 0.99
  • Mid-year discounting convention applied, with discount periods 0.5 to 8.5 years
  • Live sensitivity table: implied share price vs. WACC (8.5%–11.5%) × terminal growth rate (1.0%–3.5%), fully formula-driven
